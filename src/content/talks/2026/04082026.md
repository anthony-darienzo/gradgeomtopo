For a smooth manifold, de Rham cohomology provides a nice way to compute
cohomology groups using differential forms. The Hodge decomposition implies that
every de Rham cohomology class admits a harmonic representative. A key
ingredient in this result is the ellipticity of the Hodge Laplacian, hence the
existence of a parametrix in the pseudodifferential calculus.  On contact
manifolds, there is an analogue of the de Rham complex introduced by Rumin. It
can be shown that the resulting cohomology coincides with de Rham cohomology.
However, the associated Hodge Laplacian is no longer elliptic. To address this
issue, one must work within a different pseudodifferential framework adapted to
the geometry. This leads to the Heisenberg calculus, in which the Rumin Hodge
Laplacian admits a parametrix, hence yielding a version of the Hodge
decomposition.  In this talk, I will describe the construction of the Rumin
complex on contact manifolds and explain how Heisenberg calculus is used to
prove the hypoellipticity of the Rumin Hodge Laplacian.
